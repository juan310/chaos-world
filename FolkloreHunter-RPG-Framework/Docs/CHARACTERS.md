# Build 0.9.0 — The hunters, the skeletons and the Warden's crypt from the supplied art

The art supplied for 0.9 is kept unchanged in `Docs/Concepts/Characters-0.9/`: the main characters (male and female
hunter), the female and male hairstyle sheets (ten each), the skeleton upgrades (warrior, knight, mage), the Grave
Warden upgrade, the Warden's hallway and the Warden's lair. The older models are kept: the KayKit knight is still offered
as the "Classic knight", and every new monster falls back to the older KayKit skeleton if its new model is missing.

## Current characters: one slim body design, no chibi (owner's rule, 2026-10-27)
The Forge-made bodies on the KayKit skeleton (below) were rejected, and so was the chibi KayKit knight in any role. The
player, the skeletons and the Warden are now the Codex combat prototype's models (`Assets/Resources/Models/Codex/`, copied
unchanged from that project) on their own slim, human-proportioned skeleton, with every clip authored on that skeleton:
- `Tools/slim/build_slim.py` (Blender) makes `Models/Hunter_Male_Slim`, `Hunter_Female_Slim`, `Skel_Boneguard`,
  `Skel_IronRevenant`, `Skel_GraveHexer` and `Skel_GraveWarden` (with its separate horned helmet): the bones get the
  game's names, hand slots are added (a fist's grip: the blade on the thumb side, the edge along the knuckles), the
  weapons are seated in them at a 1.8 m hunter's size, and the clips are baked in.
- The everyday moves (idle, walk, jog, sprint, strafes, roll, back-step, hits, death, getting up, drinking, gathering,
  sitting, casting), the basic sword cuts, the leaping spin, the shield guard and bash and the bow draw are CC0 motion
  capture from the **Mesh2Motion** human animation library (github.com/scottpetrovic/mesh2motion-app, art under CC0
  1.0), retargeted by `Tools/slim/retarget.py`: torso and feet turns, hips and ankles scaled by leg length, wrists by
  arm length, the grip from the finger bones.
- The game's own moves (Thorn Rush I and II, Leap Slash, Shield Crash, the Rends, Standoff, the stab, the katana, lance
  and bow sets, the jump dash, the dive, riding) are keyposed on the slim skeleton itself (`Tools/slim/moves.py`,
  solved with two-bone IK by `Tools/slim/choreo.py`).
- The motion between those key poses is generated by **NVIDIA MotionBricks** (github.com/NVlabs/motionbricks; code
  under Apache 2.0, model weights under the NVIDIA Open Model Agreement, which allows commercial use; its sample clips
  are evaluation-only and are not used). `Tools/motionbricks/`: `mb_export.py` (Blender) poses our keyposed clips on
  MotionBricks' SOMA skeleton and records their key times; `mb_generate.py` (run in the local MotionBricks checkout on
  the RTX 5080) generates natural motion from each key pose to the next and re-times it so every key pose (a strike's
  contact frame) lands at its own time; `mb_bridge.load` brings the result back onto the slim skeleton through the same
  retargeter as the motion capture. 25 of the game's own moves are made this way (`mb_bridge.MB_CLIPS`); the poses
  and timing stay ours, MotionBricks only fills the in-between. Which gaps it fills, and with which settings, follows
  what the MotionBricks lab measured on our clips (`Docs/MOTIONBRICKS_PLAYBOOK.md`).
- Smeared flanks: the Codex bodies were reconstructed from front and back pictures, so surfaces that face sideways (the
  outsides of the legs and boots) came out smeared. `Tools/slim/paint_sides.py` repairs a body's texture: it renders the
  body in the game's standing pose, the local Forge's edit tool turns that render into left and right profiles (so they
  show the model's own armour and colours), and the profiles are projected onto only the side-facing surface each one
  sees, inside the profile's figure, baked into a copy of the texture (`build_slim.py --texture`). Done for the Grave
  Warden (`SourceArt/Repaired/GraveWarden_sides_texture.png`); the Codex originals are unchanged.
- The 3D mesh system, built into SightForge itself (Studio 0.16: `eyeslib/mesh3d.py`, `eyeslib/mesh3d_blender.py`, the
  `eyes_inspect.py mesh3d <model>` command with the standard findings and report; source and installer in
  `Tools/sightforge/`, Folklore's piece config `Tools/sightforge/folklore_mesh3d.json`; `slimall.ps1` runs it on every
  re-exported model). Its first version was the project tool `Tools/slim/mesh_check.py`: every clip of an
  exported model is played and its skinned body measured against its rest shape: triangles stretched past 1.7x or
  collapsed under 0.45x (by bone), held weapons inside the body (ray parity), the body under the floor. Thresholds are
  calibrated on the calm clips (idle, walk: 0.4-0.8 % of the vertices stretch with the cloth). It found the jump dashes,
  dodges, katana spins and leap slash sinking 8-27 cm into the ground: the clip baker now lifts any pose whose feet,
  toes or knees would go under the floor (`choreo.grounded`). `build_slim.py --smooth` softens the auto-rig's sharp
  weight hand-over at shoulders, hips and spine, measured with the same check.
- SightForge 0.17 development tools (`Tools/sightforge/README.md`): originality scan, weapon fitter, smear finder, hit
  viewer, F9 problem capture, performance capture, spring bones (follow-through on tails, ears and capes), the map and
  terrain mesh system, the foot-slide fixer (`build_slim.py --footlock` holds the ball of each planted foot still on the
  travelling ground in every walk, jog and run), and MotionBricks for any humanoid rig (in-betweens and transitions).
- Claude Eyes reviews of the live game (SightForge's AI joint finder on player pictures): `Tools/review_grips_sightforge.py`
  (the hand on the grip below the guard, every held weapon, hunters and skeletons) and `Tools/review_bow_sightforge.py`
  (the real draw and release: bow hand on the grip, bow upright, string inward, arrow on the string and across the bow,
  draw hand at the nock, anchor at the face).
- The hairstyles are the Codex prototype's strand builder (`HunterHair`), fitted to the slim head (`HairFit`).
- Review renders: `Tools/slim/preview_slim.py` (clips before export), `Tools/slim/render_fbx.py` (the exported model).

The sections below record the earlier 0.9 work; those models are kept on the side as fallbacks.

## How the models were made (all local, no paid service)
SightForge Forge on this PC (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ClaudeEyesStudio`, TripoSG shape model and FLUX.2 klein editing model on
the RTX 5080), driven by scripts in `Tools/`:
1. **Pictures.** `Tools/forge_queue.py` queues Forge jobs; `Tools/queue_09_art.ps1` lists every one made for 0.9.
   - The full-body hunters are cropped from the main character sheet (`crops/male_body.png`, `female_body.png`).
   - Back views and left/right profiles come from the edit tool ("turn the character around ...").
   - The skeletons and the Warden are edited weapon-free (fused weapons bend with the arm when animated), and their
     weapons come out as clean item pictures (the items tool, with the concept as the style reference): the warrior's
     longsword and round red shield, the knight's greatsword, the mage's staff, the Warden's greatsword.
   - The lair props (skull pedestal with brazier, sarcophagus, knight statue, candelabra, banner) are item pictures with
     the hallway picture as the style reference.
   - The hairstyles: the edit tool changes only the hair of the main character sheet's hunters, one per hairstyle on the
     sheets (`queue_09_art.ps1 -Stage hair`).
2. **3D.** The model tool turns a front picture (plus the back view) into a textured, rigged mesh; the static tool makes
   the weapons and props.
3. **Into the game's skeleton.** `Tools/build_hunter_variant.py` (Blender) puts each new body on the KayKit skeleton the
   game already animates, so every existing clip plays on it (sword, lance, bow, katana, evasions, riding, the boss
   moves): the body is scaled to the old character's height (weapons keep their in-game size), turned from the
   picture's A-pose into the KayKit rest pose, the KayKit bones are moved onto the body's joints keeping their
   directions and rolls, the clips' hip and IK movement is scaled to the longer limbs, the weights are renamed onto the
   KayKit bones. The profile pictures are projected onto the flanks the front and back pictures never saw (only surface
   that faces that side and is visible from it; baked into the texture). Monsters (`--monster`) hold their weapons
   rigidly in the hand slot the way the game's own sword and shield are held (`Docs/Assets/hand-props.json`).
   `Tools/build_09_models.ps1` runs all of it; every model writes a report to `Docs/Assets/model-variant-*.json`.

## In the game
- **Character creation** (New game → slot): male hunter, female hunter or the classic knight; the ten hairstyles of the
  chosen hunter with the sheet's portraits (`Assets/Resources/UI/Portraits`); a name (typed, or "Random name" on the
  pad). The real 3D hunter stands beside the menu in the chosen look. The choice is saved; the title screen, the
  Proving Grounds and the Training Range show the hunter last played. Saves from before 0.9 keep the classic knight.
- **The skeletons:** the Gravebound wears the skeleton warrior (sword and round shield); the new **Gravebound Knight**
  (greatsword, heavy cleaves; a slam in the hunt rules) guards the graveyard north of the crypt; the new **Gravebound
  Mage** (staff; grave bolts from range, a bone nova and curses in the hunt rules) walks the graveyard at night; two
  skeleton warriors guard the Warden's hallway.
- **The Grave Warden** wears the new armoured, horned model with its greatsword.
- **The Warden's crypt** (`Assets/Framework/Game/WardenLair.cs`): the entrance stairs; the hallway of wet cobbles
  between skull pedestals with fire braziers, crimson banners, hanging chains, candles, a barred ossuary arch and two
  knight statues; the great horned-skull door under "THE DEAD OBEY THE WARDEN" (the picture's own door and
  inscription), which the hunter pushes open; steps down into the round lair: the rune circle of ring paving, pillars,
  banners, chains, braziers, barred coffin alcoves, rubble, ground fog; the altar between two giant knight statues at
  the far end. Warm brazier light, dark stone.

## Limits (0.9.0, honest)
- The models come from single pictures: the front matches the art closely; the back and the sides are painted by the
  edit tool from the art (not drawn by an artist), and the side projection can misplace small details.
- The bodies are realistic in proportion but move with the KayKit clips (made for a chibi knight): poses read
  correctly, but arm swings and the run were not authored for these bodies.
- The hairstyles are separate models of the whole hunter (each made from its own picture), so the body can differ
  slightly from one hairstyle to another.
- Armour from the game's equipment (the Thornback set) is fitted to the classic knight only; on the new hunters it
  counts for its stats but is not shown.
- The skeletons and the Warden in game come from the reconstructions made on this PC for the Codex combat test
  (`FolkloreHunter-CombatTest-Codex/SourceArt`, read-only; `build_09_models.ps1 -Stage codexmonsters`), built onto the
  game's monster skeleton with the weapons made for 0.9; the Warden's separate horned helm is fitted over its skull and
  breaks off again in the fight.
- The crypt is built from boxes, quads, particles and the art-made props; it follows the pictures' layout and
  materials, not their exact geometry and lighting.
